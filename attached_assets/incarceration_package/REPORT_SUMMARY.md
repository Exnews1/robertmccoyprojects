## Report Overview

This comprehensive report examines United States incarceration institutions across four critical sections as requested:

### Section 1: Economics of Incarceration
- National and state-level costs ($39 billion across 40 states in 2010)
- Per-prisoner costs ($21,603 to $29,291 federal, varying by state)
- Medical and healthcare costs (10-20% of operating budgets, elderly prisoners cost 2-4x more)
- Food, operational, and other costs
- Revenue generation and prison labor
- Demographics of incarcerated population (racial disparities, aging population, gender distribution)

### Section 2: Life in Prison
- Daily life patterns and living conditions (overcrowding, triple bunking, limited access to programs)
- Gang dynamics and structures (white, African-American, Latino gangs; less than 10% control general population)
- Violence and conflict statistics (28 assaults per 1,000 inmates annually, 17-75 staff assaults per facility)
- Prisoner-guard interactions (structured conflict, 70.1% retaliation after grievances)
- Correctional staff employment and challenges (inadequate training, high stress, significant turnover)

### Section 3: Education Levels, Age, and Entry Dynamics
- Educational attainment (65% numeracy skills, 48% reading skills at or below 11-year-old level)
- Age distribution and entry patterns (average admission age 31.8 years, most offending 15-21 years)
- Pathways to incarceration (poverty, abuse, homelessness, substance misuse, mental illness)

### Section 4: Prisoner Voice, Rights, Mental Health, and Needs
- Prisoner rights and legal protections (constitutional right to adequate care, limited in practice)
- Voice mechanisms and grievance systems (widespread retaliation, code of silence)
- Mental health prevalence (16% have mental disorders, exceeding community rates)
- Mental health service delivery gaps (66-76% with mental health problems receive NO treatment)
- Prisoner needs and preferences (healthcare, safety, adequate food, programs, family contact, dignity)

## Key Statistics and Findings

### Economics
- Total taxpayer cost: $39 billion (40 states, 2010)
- Federal per-prisoner cost: $29,291 (FY2013)
- Elderly prisoner cost: $68,270 annually (2-4x average)
- Prison population growth: 790% increase (1980-2013)
- Overcrowding: 36% over rated capacity

### Violence and Safety
- 28 physical assaults per 1,000 inmates annually
- 17-75 inmate-on-staff assaults per facility per year
- 21.2% of women report sexual victimization over 6 months
- 51 inmate deaths from physical attacks (2000)
- Less than 10% of prisoners (gang members) control general population

### Education and Demographics
- 65% have numeracy skills at or below 11-year-old level
- 48% have reading skills at or below 11-year-old level
- 44.3% of inmates are Black, 36.7% White, 15.4% Hispanic
- 90% male
- Average admission age: 31.8 years

### Mental Health Crisis
- 16% have mental disorders (exceeding community rates)
- 66% of state prisoners with mental health problems receive NO treatment
- 76% of federal prisoners with mental health problems receive NO treatment
- 1-7% have severe mental disorders (psychoses)
- Up to 20% have less severe mental illnesses
