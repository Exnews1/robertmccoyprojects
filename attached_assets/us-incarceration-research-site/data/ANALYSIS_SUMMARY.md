## 💡 WHAT THIS MEANS

### If Red States Matched Blue State Rates
If the 28 red states reduced their incarceration rate from 0.8270% to match blue states' 0.6095%, they would have:
- **263,000 fewer people in prison** (a 19% reduction)
- Savings of billions in corrections costs
- Rate would drop from 8.3 per 1,000 to 6.1 per 1,000

### Geographic Patterns
- **Southern red states** (LA, MS, AL, AR, GA) have particularly high rates
- **Northeastern blue states** (MN, NJ, NY, CT, MD, MA) have particularly low rates
- **Western states** show more variation regardless of party

## 🔍 METHODOLOGY

**Political Party Classification:**
- **Red (Republican):** State has Republican trifecta (Governor + House + Senate)
- **Blue (Democratic):** State has Democratic trifecta (Governor + House + Senate)
- **Purple (Split):** Mixed party control

**Data Sources:**
- U.S. Census Bureau (2024) - Population estimates
- Prison Policy Initiative (2024) - Incarceration data
- Ballotpedia (2024) - Political party control

**Calculation:**
```
% Incarcerated = (Prison Population ÷ State Population) × 100
```

---

## ⚠️ IMPORTANT NOTES

1. **Prison vs. Jail:** This analysis covers **prisons** (state/federal facilities for sentenced individuals), not jails (local facilities for pre-trial/short sentences)

2. **Correlation ≠ Causation:** Political party control correlates with incarceration rates but doesn't prove causation. Many factors influence rates including:
   - Historical policies
   - Crime rates
   - Socioeconomic factors
   - Judicial practices
   - Drug policy approaches

3. **Snapshot in Time:** Data represents 2024 and may not reflect very recent policy changes
