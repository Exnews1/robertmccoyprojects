What the platform does well:
The governance framing is genuinely strong and consistently applied across every module. The NIST AI RMF 1.0 MAP compliance table in Part B is the single most technically impressive artifact — it is the kind of documentation that earns credibility with a policy and research audience immediately. The Credential Sequencing Engine is the clearest demonstration of the deterministic rules engine concept, and the Reference Explorer produces grounded, citation-backed answers with appropriate epistemic humility. The research corpus (797 sources, four-document series) establishes scholarly depth.
The critical problems:
There are three issues that, if unaddressed, will damage the demonstration regardless of how good the underlying concept is. First, multiple interactive elements are non-functional — the "View Full Case" buttons, the Approve/Modify/Escalate actions, and the scenario chat interface produce no visible output. A live demo with dead buttons is fatal. Second, the platform is fragmented across three subdomains with inconsistent design, which makes it read as a collection of student projects rather than an integrated system. Third, Part A (the Service Member Hub) does not exist, which means the three-layer architecture cannot be demonstrated end-to-end.
The six-week roadmap in plain terms:
Weeks 1–2: Fix every broken link, button, and 404. Relabel "Live Data" as "Demonstration Data." Replace illustrative placeholder references with real NICE/O*NET citations.
Weeks 2–3: Unify all components under a single domain with consistent navigation and design.
Weeks 3–4: Build a lightweight simulation backend so that scenario runs, skill translations, and ISR actions feel live and produce state changes.
Weeks 4–5: Build a minimal Service Member Hub and an interactive Audit Trail Explorer to complete the narrative arc.
Weeks 5–6: Add the Policy Gap Visualizer, a Guided Demo Mode, and expand the Credential Engine to cover healthcare and logistics domains. Full rehearsal.
The highest-impact conference feature that does not yet exist:
A real-time Governance Transparency Panel that shows, during any AI-assisted operation, which data signals are being consulted, which rules are being evaluated, and where human review is required — updating live as the system processes a request. This makes the governance architecture visible rather than merely described, which is the difference between telling the audience the system is trustworthy and showing them why.
