MODULE OVERVIEW

Welcome to Module 3 of your Meridian Industrial Group new employee
orientation. This module introduces you to the organization's Knowledge
Management System (KMS), your responsibilities as a knowledge
contributor, and the tools you will use daily to access, create, and
manage organizational knowledge.

By the end of this module, you will be able to:
  - Navigate the Meridian Knowledge Repository
  - Understand the document classification taxonomy
  - Submit documents through the intake process
  - Locate policies, procedures, and reference materials
  - Recognize your role in maintaining knowledge quality

------------------------------------------------------------------------

SECTION 1: WHY KNOWLEDGE MANAGEMENT MATTERS

Organizations lose significant productivity when critical information is
trapped in individual inboxes, personal drives, or the institutional
memory of employees who have moved on. Meridian's KMS exists to solve
this problem by creating a single, governed, searchable repository
where organizational knowledge lives and remains accessible regardless
of personnel changes.

As a new employee, you benefit from the KMS immediately. Instead of
searching through email threads or interrupting colleagues, you can
find the current version of any policy, procedure, or reference
document through the Knowledge Repository. This saves time, reduces
errors, and ensures you are always working from the most current
information.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

SECTION 2: THE KNOWLEDGE REPOSITORY

The Knowledge Repository is Meridian's central document library. Every
document in the repository has been through a governed intake process
that includes automated classification, human review, and approval.
This means you can trust that documents in the repository are current,
properly categorized, and officially sanctioned.

Documents are organized by type:
  - Policies (POL): Governing rules and compliance frameworks
  - Standard Operating Procedures (SOP): Step-by-step process guides
  - Training Materials (TRN): Instructional and onboarding content
  - Reports (RPT): Analysis, audits, and performance summaries
  - Memoranda (MEM): Internal communications and directives
  - Specifications (SPE): Technical requirements and standards
  - Contracts (CON): Agreements and service-level documents
  - Reference (REF): Lookup tables, glossaries, directories

Each document follows a standard naming convention so you can quickly
identify what a document is, what it covers, and when it was created,
without needing to open it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

SECTION 3: SUBMITTING DOCUMENTS

Any employee may submit a document for inclusion in the Knowledge
Repository. The process is straightforward:

  1. Navigate to the Document Intelligence Engine intake portal.
  2. Upload your document (PDF, DOCX, or TXT format, 10 MB maximum).
  3. The system will automatically classify your document and assign
     a suggested type, subject, and standard name.
  4. A reviewer from the Knowledge Management Office will examine
     the classification, and either approve, modify, or reject the
     submission.
  5. Approved documents appear in the Knowledge Repository and become
     available to all authorized employees.

Important: Do not upload documents containing Tier 4 (Restricted)
information through the standard intake portal. Restricted documents
require a separate submission process coordinated through the Chief
Data Officer's office.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

SECTION 4: YOUR RESPONSIBILITIES

As a Meridian employee, you are expected to:
  - Consult the Knowledge Repository before creating new documents
    to avoid duplication
  - Submit documents that have organizational value through the
    intake process rather than storing them only in personal drives
  - Report outdated or inaccurate documents to your division's
    Data Steward
  - Complete annual knowledge management refresher training

Knowledge is a shared asset. When you contribute to the repository,
you strengthen the entire organization's ability to operate effectively,
make informed decisions, and maintain continuity through personnel
transitions.
