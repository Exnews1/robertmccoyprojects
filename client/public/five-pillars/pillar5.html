Pillar 5: Veteran & Servicemember Learner Voice

Evidence from 188 peer-reviewed sources (2015-2026)

Executive Summary

Veteran and service-member learners face layered institutional, financial, social, and psychological barriers that interact with identity shifts from soldier to student; targeted cohort models, veteran centers, cohort-based STEM supports, advising, and peer networks show measurable breakthroughs but evidence on funding effects and some program impacts is mixed.

Layer 2: Evidence Synthesis

  • Population scale: More than 9,000 recently separated post-9/11 veterans were followed; a majority obtained a postsecondary degree within four years of separation.
  • Institutional cohort data: In one veteran-friendly institution (n=1,141), enrollment patterns and aid sources predicted graduation and persistence.
  • Health and hidden wounds: Large administrative counts reported over 300,000 TBI cases (2000-2014) and surveys documented high rates of PTSD symptoms.

Barriers & Identity Conflicts

Veteran students encounter interlocking institutional, financial, social, and psychological barriers while negotiating military-to-student identity transitions.

Key Finding: Barriers are not additive only; they interact - financial strain amplifies stress, institutional opacity increases isolation.

Layer 2: Intervention Evidence

  • Cohort models and peer networks: Cohort programs improved transition experiences and were associated with better integration into campus culture.
  • STEM scholarship cohort success: A mixed-methods evaluation reported an 80% degree completion rate, approximately 24 percentage points higher than the institution's six-year average.
  • Veterans resource centers: Campus veterans centers, VA benefits certification support, and career services were statistically associated with higher veteran success scores.

Retention, Nontraditional Challenges & Policy Directions

Retention and persistence are shaped by enrollment intensity, aid source, sense of community, and nontraditional characteristics such as caregiving and first-generation status.

Layer 2: Policy Framework

Key Determinants
  • Retention: Enrollment patterns and aid source uniquely predict veteran persistence.
  • Sense of community: Connectedness often emerges as the strongest predictor.
  • First-generation burdens: Veterans commonly balance family and work responsibilities.
Policy Recommendations
  • Standardize VA-campus processes: Reduce enrollment friction.
  • Fund tailored program design: Tie resources to proven practices.
  • Scale peer and cohort models: Include multi-site comparisons.

Concluding Synthesis

Veteran learners possess resilience and vocationally relevant skills yet face systemic frictions that lower the efficiency of higher-education trajectories. Evidence supports comprehensive, learner-centered programs - cohorts, veteran resource centers, advising, and tailored accommodations - as the most promising breakthroughs, though rigorous causal evaluations are limited.
