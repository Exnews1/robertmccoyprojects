Military Learner Career Mobility

Pillar 1 | 174 peer-reviewed sources (2015-2026)

Executive Summary

Post-9/11 veterans show mixed civilian employment and earnings outcomes: higher education uptake but variable job matches, persistent underemployment for some groups, and rising disability payments. Rigorous interventions and credential translation improve employment; policy should target skill alignment and benefit navigation.

Transition Outcomes & Evidence

Longitudinal and quasi-experimental studies report both modest earnings premiums for some subgroups and elevated unemployment or underemployment risks for others.

RCT Evidence Quasi-Exp

Detailed Research Synthesis

  • Enrollment and degree effects: Post-9/11 GI Bill recipients increased months enrolled and marginally raised bachelor's attainment (+1.2 pp) while showing a small negative effect on average annual earnings nine years post-separation.
  • RCT employment gains: Intensive multifaceted employment coaching raised employment rates (95% vs 83% at two years) and cumulative income (~$16,872 greater).
  • Unemployment comparisons: Post-9/11 veterans were more likely to be unemployed than comparable nonveterans (7.2% vs 6.0% in some CPS-based work).

Barriers & MOS Transferability

Transition barriers cluster into credentialing, employer perception gaps, health-related constraints, and access/navigation obstacles.

Critical: Military-specific MOS tasks often lack civilian analogues.

Barrier Analysis

Credential Mismatch

Civilian employers often require licenses that don't map directly to MOS training.

Skill Translation Gaps

Veterans and employers report difficulty translating military tasks into civilian job descriptions.

Health Impacts

Disability compensation and service-connected conditions complicate employment trajectories.

Program Access

Many programs don't offer the barrier-reduction components veterans find most helpful.

Benefits Utilization Analysis

  • Degree and earnings tradeoffs: While PGIB increased college participation, some groups experienced worse longer-run earnings trajectories.
  • Sector effects: Veterans from for-profit institutions frequently graduate with higher debt burdens.
  • Vocational training: Veterans who used benefits for job-specific training realized faster labor market attachment.

Theoretical Frameworks

Scholars apply human capital, signaling, and life course frameworks to explain veterans' career mobility.

Theoretical & Methodological Analysis

Theoretical Perspectives
  • Human capital: Training as investments; transferability depends on specificity
  • Signaling: Civilian credentials required to signal competence
  • Life course: Multiple life domains interacting with employment
Methodological Approaches
  • RCTs: Causal evidence for employment interventions
  • Quasi-experimental: Administrative records for long-run trends
  • Longitudinal surveys: TVMI, B&B for multi-domain tracking
